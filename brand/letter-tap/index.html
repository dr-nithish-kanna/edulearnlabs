Letter Station: art direction preview

Static prototype for approval (game 6, Letter tap, Pre-K). Nothing here is the final game and there is no game logic. It shows the cast, the station, the letter carriers, every state, the Alphabet train reward, and the phone and desktop views, so we can agree on the art before any development. It follows the v3 style guide: cream and teal, rounded shapes, soft tinted shadows, no red in feedback. Letters are set in Andika, the kit's early-reader font, and titles in Fredoka (both embedded from the website's self-hosted copies, so what you see is what the game will show). Motion is gentle, all transform and opacity, with a reduced-motion version.

1. The cast: the fox station master and Hazel

A fox runs the station. He is orange with a cream face and chest, big ears with dark tips, a bushy tail, and a teal jacket with a sun-yellow scarf, buttons and a station master's cap with a star badge (the star links to the stars children earn, like Hazel's charm). He has the same chibi proportions as Hazel: big head, big eyes, round cheeks, soft outlines in a darker shade of his own colour. He carries a plain green "go" paddle, so there is no letter or numeral on him anywhere. Hazel stays as the host and hint helper, exactly as drawn in brand/mascot/owl.svg.

Readable at phone size

The fox is drawn 170 scene px wide, which is 106 px on a 667 x 375 phone. Below he is shown at 64 px, the smallest size we allow, next to his happy pose. The cap, ears, eyes and tail still read at 64 px, and happy versus neutral is clear from the open mouth and raised arms alone.

2. The station scene (no letters anywhere)

A small railway station with a flat teal canopy roof, a blank signboard, a door, two windows with flower boxes, a platform with a yellow safety stripe, and a train of five empty cars waiting on the top track behind the platform. Each solved letter fills one car (the dashed windows are where the letter and its picture land). The Back button, ten progress dots and audio toggle follow the same HUD as the other games.

Rule check (F9): no letters, numerals or letter-shaped props. What is in the scene: a house with a flat roof (not a gable, which reads as A), a blank signboard (no text, no clock face), a lantern in a rounded box (not a hanging lamp), round-topped bushes, flat horizontal rails and sleepers. What was left out on purpose: a station clock (an O and numerals), lamp posts and telegraph poles (I and T), pointed mountains (A), a signal arm (T or Y), a horseshoe (U) and railings (a row of I). The only circles are the solid train wheels and the flower dots, which no child will read as the letter O.

3. The letter carriers (parcels)

One flat shape for every letter: a suitcase-style parcel with a handle and four rivets, a white label face and a big upright letter in Andika. Seven colours from the style guide, used in rotation by the planner so neighbours differ. The ink letter always sits on the light label (8:1 contrast or better), never white on a bright colour. The colour of a parcel carries no meaning, so a child cannot learn "the pink one is M".

Anatomy

The box may tilt up to about 6 degrees in a pile (the planner's rotation times 0.3). The letter is a separate layer drawn on top and is never rotated, so an N never reads as Z and an M never reads as W.

Level 1: a row of 3 (S, A, M)

Big, spread out, easy. The fox asks and the bubble shows only a speaker (the request is spoken, never written, so the child matches a name to a shape, not a shape to a shape). Centres are 170 scene px apart. No look-alikes at this level, from the locked pool A B C D E M O S.

Level 4: a pile of 8 with three look-alike pairs

Eight parcels in three loose rows with tiny tilts. The board holds the look-alike pairs M and W, E and F, O and Q, plus H and S. Every centre is at least 112 scene px from every other (closest here: 115), parcels are 104 px, so nothing touches or covers another. The letters are never rotated. Here the board is waiting at the idle shimmer: the sheen sweeps over all eight in reading order, so it points at nothing. Three cars are already filled.

4. The states

Muted players still get everything: the speaker pulse is the visual twin of the spoken request, the dim is the twin of "That's W.", and the hop and glow are the twin of the note and the voice.

Asking: a speaker bubble, no letter shown

The speaker disc is 104 scene px (65 px on a phone) and taps to hear the request again, free. It pulses softly while the voice speaks. See levels 1 and 4 above.

Wrong tap: the letter dims, the fox thinks

The tapped W dims and keeps its letter visible. The fox plays the thinking pose and Hazel tilts her head (in the game only her head moves; here the whole owl leans a little because the preview uses her fixed art). The voice says "That's W. Find M!". No red, no cross, no counter.

Narrowed to two

After the second miss every parcel dims except the answer and one planned rival, here the look-alike W. "M or W?" is the exact discrimination the top level exists to teach. After a third miss only the answer stays lit, and the child still makes the last tap.

Found: hop and glow

The M parcel hops at the tap, glows with sparkles and a warm ring, the fox goes happy, and the bubble now shows the picture and "M is for moon" (the letter may be shown now that it is solved). The newest car at the top fills with M and a moon. The other parcels dim so the eye stays on the winner.

Muted: the card hint

If the player has audio off, they cannot hear the request, so the bubble shows the letter card ("It looks like this!") from the start, and the audio toggle shows a slash. The same card appears when a child taps Hazel for help while audio is on. Showing it costs one hint.

5. Reward: the Alphabet train

One car per solved round, with the letter and a picture. The fox's train grows by rounds solved, never by time. Cars are coloured in rotation, the letter is in Andika, and the picture is a simple flat SVG in the same style as the rest of the art. The word is never shown on the car (reading load stays minimal); it is written below for the owner only.

The train on the end screen (engine plus the 8 newest cars)

The 26 pictures

One picturable word per letter. Each starts with its plain letter and that letter's common first sound. X is "box", with the x at the end of the word (shown in pink below); the box picture has no cross or X-shaped tape on it, on purpose. The teal letter is the one the word starts with. Words to look at twice: Q "quilt" (starts with "kw", which is Q's normal sound), I "igloo" and O "octopus" (short vowel sounds, not "ice" or "owl"), U "umbrella" (short u), J "jam" (a jam jar) and E "egg".

7. Reduced motion and muted play

Reduced motion. Every motion on this page is transform or opacity only. When the device asks for reduced motion: the parcel pop-in becomes a fade, the hop becomes a glow (opacity), the shimmer becomes a steady soft sheen (opacity only), the speaker pulse becomes a still ring, the fox's bob stops, the new car fades in place instead of rolling in, and confetti is off. The state still shows: a found parcel is still lit and glowing, a wrong one is still dimmed. Try it:

Normal: the parcel hops with a spring, a glow and sparkles appear.

8. Decisions for the owner

  • Carrier (decision 18). One flat suitcase-style parcel in seven colours, letter on a white label, only the box tilts. Say yes, or ask for a plain card or a different object (a crate, a gift box).
  • Fox likeness. Orange fox, cream face, teal jacket, sun scarf, station master cap with a star. His teal jacket is the same teal as the Back button so the brand colour sits on a new friend. Hazel's brown and the fox's orange are different enough that they never blur, and Hazel stays in her corner on a crate.
  • Reward words (decision 19). A apple, B ball, C cat, D dog, E egg, F fish, G grapes, H house, I igloo, J jam, K kite, L lemon, M moon, N nest, O octopus, P pig, Q quilt, R rainbow, S sun, T turtle, U umbrella, V vase, W worm, X box, Y yarn, Z zebra. Swap any you dislike; each is one small SVG.
  • Train placement. The scene shows the train of empty cars on a top track so the goal is visible while playing. The design doc described a bottom strip with the newest 8 cars; this preview puts the five-car train at the top instead (the end screen shows the 8-car strip). Either works; the top track keeps the bottom free for the pile.
  • Words on cars. The cars show the letter and the picture only, no word. The word is spoken ("M is for moon"). Say if you want the word printed on the car too.
  • Palette. No new colours. The fox orange (#F28C38) and dark tail brown (#6E3B22) are new tints near the existing caramel and cocoa; the parcel colours reuse sky, pink, sun, leaf and teal plus an orange (#FF9F43) and a lilac (#B69CF0) already used for the duck feet and mouse.
  • Small hit areas. Back and audio need a padded hit area (about 48 px); Hazel needs about 64 px.
