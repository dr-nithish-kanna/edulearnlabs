Hedgehog Picnic: art direction preview

Static prototype for approval. Nothing here is the final game. It shows the cast, the counting objects, the four level layouts, the counting moment, the How many? step, the Done bell and the Parade reward, so we can agree on the art before any development. Gentle motion only (bobbing, hop, twinkle), all transform and opacity, with a reduced-motion version. It follows the v3 style guide: cream and teal, Fredoka numbers, rounded shapes, soft tinted shadows, no red in feedback. This page uses a rounded system font where the real game self-hosts Fredoka, so numeral widths are close but not exact.

1. The cast: hedgehog, duck, mouse

Hedgehog is the host and asks for apples. Duck asks for pebbles. Mouse asks for acorns. Each has a neutral pose (waiting, gentle bob) and a happy pose (closed smiling eyes, open mouth, arms or wings up) that plays when a round is solved. Same chibi proportions as Hazel: big head, big eyes, round cheeks, soft outlines in a darker shade of the body colour.

2. The countable objects

Drawn at 104 scene px, which is 65 px on a 667 x 375 phone, just above the 64 px hit target. The three silhouettes differ on purpose: the apple is round with a leaf, the pebble is flat and wide, the acorn is tall and pointed. The silhouette and greyscale versions are there to prove they still read for a child who cannot tell the colours apart.

3. Level layouts

Level 1: one neat row of 5 (hedgehog and apples)

Count phase, three apples tapped. The order bubble has a picture and two short words, "Count the apples". The gingham picnic blanket is the table: pale so the objects pop. Hazel sits on a stump in the corner as the hint helper (tap her for a hint). The bottom shelf shows the parade so far.

Level 2: two neat rows, 5 + 5 (duck and pebbles)

Rows are 112 scene px apart and objects are 118 px apart across, so the badge and rings never reach a neighbour. Seven pebbles are counted, the badge sits on number 7.

Level 3: a scatter of 8 (mouse and acorns)

Eight acorns thrown loosely across the blanket with slight tilts. It is a scatter, not a heap: every centre is at least 130 scene px from every other, and objects are 104 px, so nothing touches or hides another. Skipping one is easy, which is the point. A Done bell (104 px) waits beside the mouse.

4. The counting moment

One badge for the whole round, always on top, never blocking a tap. It is 84 scene px across with a 60 px numeral (52 px and 38 px on a phone), sun yellow with a white rim. It sits centred on the object that was just tapped. A tapped object hops, then keeps a warm ring under it. Counting is not right or wrong, so there is never a tick.

Muted players still get everything: the badge pop is the visual twin of the count note and the spoken number, and the ring is the visual twin of "that one is done".

5. The "How many?" step

When the last object is counted, the badge fades, the friend asks, and three big choices bounce into the shelf slot where the parade was. Each choice is 132 x 104 scene px (82 x 65 on a phone) with a 76 px numeral. The friend waits with a slow bob.

After a first wrong choice that choice dims (it stays visible, not tappable). No red, no X, no buzzer. The friend tilts their head, and the apples light up one after another, each lifting a little, in the order the child counted. This is a silent count-along. Here the third apple is mid-light.

6. Level 3 Done bell and "find the rest"

Level 3 has no automatic finish: the child rings the bell when they think they have counted everything. If objects are left, the uncounted ones twinkle (soft yellow glow plus sparkles, opacity only), the bell gives a little wobble and the mouse asks "Did we count them all?". Here six acorns are counted and two still twinkle.

7. Level 4: "give me N"

Uses the existing drag-and-drop fill mechanic. The bubble shows the number and a picture, "Give me 6". The crate holds the apples (each 104 px, big enough to grab), the basket on the right shows a count badge, one apple is lifting mid-drag, and the Done bell (104 px) appears after the first apple goes in. The numeral stays on screen because it is the request, not the answer.

8. Reward: Parade of animals

Six baby farm animals, one new animal joins the parade per solved round, in a fixed order that repeats. Hedgehog, duck and mouse are never in the parade: they are the hosts, the animals are the reward. The six, in cycle order:

10. Reduced motion and muted play

Reduced motion. Every motion on this page is transform or opacity only. When the device asks for reduced motion, the hop, bob, bell wobble, parade march and sparkle pulse stop. The state still shows: a counted object gets its ring instantly, the badge simply fades in (no spring), a twinkle is a steady soft glow, the animal appears in the parade with a fade. Try it:

11. Decisions for the owner

  • Palette. The page uses the v3 tokens. Two additions to approve: a soft warm grey-lilac for the mouse (#D9D2E3), and a pale teal gingham for the blanket (#BFE6E1 on white). Apples are red (#EF5B45). The style guide bans red in feedback, not in objects, and the ladybug already set that precedent. Say if you want green apples instead.
  • Cast likeness. Hedgehog has a cream face and a layered brown spiky back. Duck is sun yellow with a sky blue bow (so it never looks like the chick). Mouse is lilac grey with big pink ears. All three share Hazel's chibi look. Hedgehog brown is close to Hazel's brown, so Hazel stays on her stump in the corner as a different shape.
  • Parade species. Chick, lamb, calf, piglet, bunny, kid goat. Each has a different silhouette: round ball, cloud wool, horns and spots, floppy pink ears, tall ears, small horns and beard. The chick is the only one close to the duck, which is why the duck wears a bow. Swap the chick for a foal if you see them as the same.
  • Front view. All animals face the viewer (like Hazel) so they march in place with a bob. A side view would need new art per animal.
  • Crate stock. The level 4 crate shows only five apples. The real stock depends on the fill mechanic. If it is bigger than what fits, the crate needs a heaped look or the apples need to refill.
  • Words in bubbles. "Count the apples", "How many apples?", "Count with me!", "Did we count them all?" and "Give me 6". All short, all spoken as well.
  • Small buttons. Back and audio need a padded hit area (about 48 px) when built.
